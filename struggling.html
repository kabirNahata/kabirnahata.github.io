What I'm struggling with

This page exists because I'm tired of pretending learning is smooth. It's not. Most days are messy, confusing, and filled with doubt. If I only share the wins, I'm lying about what growth actually looks like.

So here it is: the real, unpolished truth about where I'm stuck right now. Maybe you'll relate. Maybe you have advice. Either way, this is me learning to be honest about the hard parts.

Last updated: January 9, 2025

Technical struggles

I don't really understand pointers (still)

I can write code with pointers. I can pass the CS50 problem sets. But if you asked me to explain why I need a pointer to a pointer in certain situations, I'd have to Google it. Again.

The syntax makes sense. The concept makes sense. But when I'm debugging a segfault at 11 PM, I'm still just trying things until it works. That's not understanding—that's guessing.

What I'm doing about it: Drawing memory diagrams. Forcing myself to trace execution on paper before running code. It's slow and frustrating, but I think it's the only way.

The gap between tutorials and building from scratch

I can follow along with a tutorial and feel like I get it. Then I close the video, open a blank file, and... freeze. Where do I even start? How do I structure this? What should I name this function?

I've built maybe 10 "projects," but most are just slightly modified tutorials. The handful I've tried to build truly from scratch turned into spaghetti code I was too embarrassed to commit.

What I'm doing about it: Forcing myself to build tiny tools without looking anything up. Even if they're ugly. Even if no one sees them. Getting comfortable with messy first drafts.

Math for machine learning feels like a wall

I loved Andrew Ng's ML course. The intuition clicked. But when I tried to go deeper (reading research papers, understanding transformers), I hit a wall of linear algebra and calculus I don't have.

I can follow the derivations during lectures, but I can't generate them myself. It feels like I'm learning recipes without understanding chemistry.

What I'm doing about it: Working through "Linear Algebra Done Right" slowly. Implementing everything in NumPy. Trying to build the foundations I skipped by going straight to ML.

I write code that works, not code that's good

My code is... functional. But messy. Variable names like temp and x2. Functions that do three things instead of one. Comments that apologize instead of explain.

I know what "clean code" is supposed to look like. I've read the articles. But when I'm in the flow of solving a problem, readability goes out the window. Then I come back a week later and can't understand my own code.

What I'm doing about it: Reading other people's code more. Refactoring old projects. Forcing myself to write docstrings before I write the function, not after.

Mental & emotional struggles

Imposter syndrome is loud

Some days I feel like I'm faking it. I see people my age (or younger) winning hackathons, interning at FAANG companies, publishing papers. Meanwhile, I just spent three days debugging a segmentation fault that turned out to be a typo.

I know comparison is toxic. I know everyone's on their own timeline. But late at night, when I'm stuck on something "simple," the voice that says "you're not smart enough for this" gets really loud.

What helps: Tracking small wins. Rereading code I wrote 3 months ago and realizing how much I've learned. Reminding myself that everyone feels like this sometimes.

Self-directed learning is lonely

I miss having classmates. When I'm stuck, there's no one to turn to and say "do you get this?" No study groups. No shared frustration over a hard problem set. Just me, documentation, and Stack Overflow.

Online communities help, but it's not the same. Everyone's at different levels, working on different things. I feel like I'm learning in a vacuum.

What I'm trying: Finding Discord servers for learners. Posting my struggles publicly (like this page). Considering local meetups once I'm confident enough.

The fear of "wasting time" on the wrong things

Should I be learning web development? Systems programming? Machine learning? Every time I commit to one path, I worry I'm missing out on something else. What if I spend a year mastering C and then realize everyone's hiring for JavaScript?

I know fundamentals transfer. I know there's no "right" path. But the anxiety of making wrong decisions when I don't have a formal curriculum is real.

What I'm doing about it: Giving myself permission to explore. Setting 3-month focus areas instead of trying to plan my entire learning journey. Trusting that confusion is part of the process.

Motivation is unreliable

Some weeks I'm on fire—coding for hours, making progress, feeling like I'm finally getting it. Other weeks? I open VSCode, stare at my project, and close it. The excitement fades, the grind feels heavy, and I wonder if I even like this.

I'm learning that motivation isn't constant. Discipline matters more. But on the days when both are gone? Those are hard.

What helps: Lowering the bar. "Just read one page" instead of "study for two hours." Celebrating tiny wins. Remembering why I started this in the first place.

Structural struggles (being 16 with an unconventional path)

Explaining this choice to others is exhausting

"So you're not going to school?"
"What will you do for college?"
"Isn't that risky?"
"Don't you need a degree?"

I've gotten better at explaining my choice, but it's tiring to constantly justify it. Most people don't understand self-directed learning. They think I'm either a genius (I'm not) or making a huge mistake (I hope not).

What helps: Focusing on people who get it. Building in public so I have proof of progress. Reminding myself I don't owe anyone an explanation for my life.

Uncertainty about the future

Most days I'm confident I'm on the right path. But sometimes—usually at 2 AM—I panic. What if this doesn't work? What if I can't get into a good university? What if I'm unemployable without a traditional background?

I don't have a safety net. I'm betting on myself in a way that feels both empowering and terrifying.

What helps: Focusing on skills, not credentials. Building a portfolio that speaks louder than a transcript. Trusting that people who value learning will see the value in this path.

Why I'm sharing this: Not for sympathy, but for honesty. If you're struggling too—with learning, with doubt, with feeling behind—you're not alone. Growth is messy. Real learning involves a lot of confusion, frustration, and feeling stuck.

This page will evolve as I do. Some struggles will resolve. New ones will appear. But I'll keep updating it, because pretending everything is smooth helps no one.

If any of this resonated, or if you have advice, I'd genuinely love to hear from you. Find me on LinkedIn or GitHub.
