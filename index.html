Learning in public,
one day at a time.

I'm a 16-year-old from Nepal choosing self-directed learning over traditional school. This is my space to reflect, document, and share what I'm building—not to impress, but to understand. No polish, just progress.

Why I'm Here

The path less traveled

After completing SEE, I made an unconventional choice: skip +2 and teach myself computer science. Not because I'm against formal education, but because I wanted freedom to learn deeply, explore widely, and build things that matter to me.

Most days, I feel behind. I see peers with structured curriculums while I'm piecing together my own path through online courses, documentation, and trial-and-error. But I'm learning that real understanding doesn't come from racing through content—it comes from sitting with confusion until it becomes clarity.

This journal isn't a highlight reel. It's an honest record of learning: the breakthroughs, the dead ends, the concepts that click at 2 AM, and the ones that still don't make sense after weeks. If you're also learning in the margins, maybe you'll find something here that resonates.

Guiding Principles

How I'm learning

Depth over breadth

I'd rather understand one concept thoroughly than check off ten topics superficially. It's slower, sometimes frustrating, but it sticks.

Building as learning

The best way I learn is by breaking things and fixing them. Every project is a lesson in disguise—even the ones that never get finished.

Reflection compounds

Writing forces clarity. When I explain what I learned—or what confused me—I understand it better. This journal is as much for me as anyone else.

Struggling is part of it

I used to hide when I didn't understand something. Now I'm trying to be honest about where I'm stuck. Growth lives in that discomfort.

What I've Studied

Courses & reflections

Completed • Winter 2025

CS50x: Introduction to Computer Science

Harvard's legendary intro course. What struck me most wasn't the languages (C, Python, SQL, JavaScript) but how it taught me to think computationally. Weeks 3–5 (algorithms and memory) broke my brain in the best way. I finally understood why pointers matter and how abstraction actually works.

What I'm still wrestling with: Dynamic memory allocation. I can implement it, but I don't yet have the intuition for when to use malloc vs. stack allocation in real scenarios.

Completed • January 2025

Stanford Code in Place

My first formal programming course. Python basics, decomposition, and building something real (my final project was a quiz game). The section groups felt like a tiny classroom, which I didn't expect to value so much as a self-learner.

Biggest takeaway: Programming isn't about memorizing syntax—it's about breaking problems into smaller problems until they're trivial. That mindset shift changed everything.

Completed • Winter 2024

Supervised Machine Learning: Regression and Classification

Andrew Ng's course on Coursera. Learned the fundamentals: gradient descent, cost functions, logistic regression. The math was intimidating at first, but implementing algorithms from scratch in NumPy made it click. There's something magical about watching a model learn.

Where I'm stuck: I can follow the derivations during lectures, but I can't yet derive cost functions on my own. Need to spend more time with the linear algebra.

Honest Reflections

What I'm struggling with right now

Imposter syndrome is loud this week. I spent three days debugging a segmentation fault in a C program only to realize I was freeing memory twice. Three days. Meanwhile, I see people on Twitter casually shipping production ML models. The comparison trap is real.

I'm also realizing how much I don't know about data structures. I can implement a linked list, but ask me when to use one over an array in production? I'm guessing. Textbook knowledge vs. practical intuition—that gap feels huge right now.
